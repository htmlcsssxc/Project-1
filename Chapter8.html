Unit 8: Information Security and Cyber Law

Digital society: A digital society refers to a community that heavily relies on digital technologies for communication, education, business, and daily activities. It has transformed how people interact, work, and access information, but also raises concerns about privacy, security, and digital literacy.

Computer ethics: Computer ethics refers to the moral principles and guidelines that govern the responsible and appropriate use of computers and technology. It addresses issues such as respecting privacy, avoiding piracy, preventing harm through misuse of technology, and promoting honest and fair digital behavior.

Information security: Information security involves protecting data, systems, and networks from unauthorized access, misuse, disclosure, or destruction. It includes practices such as encryption, access control, and authentication to ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of information.

Cybercrime: Cybercrime refers to illegal activities carried out using computers or the internet, such as hacking, identity theft, online fraud, and data breaches. These crimes can cause significant financial, personal, and organizational harm, making cybersecurity awareness essential.

Malware: Malware, short for malicious software, includes harmful programs such as viruses, worms, trojans, ransomware, and spyware designed to damage, disrupt, or gain unauthorized access to computer systems. Understanding malware types helps in developing effective protection strategies.

Protection: Protection methods refer to the tools and practices used to safeguard computer systems from cyber threats, including firewalls, antivirus software, regular software updates, strong passwords, and safe browsing habits to prevent unauthorized access and data loss.

Intellectual property rights: Intellectual property rights are legal protections granted to creators and inventors for their original works, including software, designs, and content. These rights, such as copyrights and patents, prevent unauthorized use, copying, or distribution of creative and technical work.

Digital signature: A digital signature is an electronic method used to verify the authenticity and integrity of digital documents or messages. It uses cryptographic techniques to ensure that the content has not been altered and confirms the identity of the sender.

Cyber law in Nepal: Cyber law in Nepal refers to the legal framework established to regulate internet use, electronic transactions, and cybercrime within the country. It defines legal provisions for addressing offenses such as hacking, data theft, and online fraud, ensuring accountability in digital activities.

ICT policy: ICT (Information and Communication Technology) policy refers to government guidelines and strategies aimed at promoting the development, use, and regulation of technology within a country. It focuses on improving digital infrastructure, education, and access to technology for national development.
